Crema 1.6.0

A polish release, and a large one: everything a round of bug hunting turned up across the three surfaces, the Settings window and the system border, plus the maintenance fixes that had been waiting since the last release. Nothing about how the app is set up changed.

Fixed

  • A track with no length no longer draws a full bar. Radio and live streams showed the progress bar sweeping to 100% in the first second and staying there, as if the track had ended; the bar now stays empty for content that has no duration, and the elapsed time keeps one shape instead of widening in place when a stream crosses an hour.
  • The compact player still says "playing" under Reduce Motion or Low Power Mode. With either on, the waveform stopped moving and lost the only signal that distinguished playing from paused; it now holds a still, staggered silhouette while playing and a flat one while paused.
  • The notch surface sits exactly in the slit. One rule now finds the slit from its edges, so the surface, the click zone and the slit always coincide — half a point of the surface used to land on live menu-bar pixels on some geometries. A display that reports a safe area but no cutout is no longer treated as a notch, which used to hand the whole menu bar's clicks to Crema while music played.
  • The title column in the notch gained room. A spacer was charging a double gap on the narrowest surface in the app; the title now takes the flexible role and keeps eight to ten more points.
  • The speaker icon stays put while the volume moves. The glyph slid up to four points as the waves grew; it is anchored to its leading edge now, in every style.
  • Dragging a brightness bar backed by BetterDisplay is more trustworthy. The bar only anchors to levels confirmed on the wire — including its own echo on the way back round, which used to pass for a reading — and if the channel fails mid-drag the fallback writes the exact level your finger stopped at, never one it passed through. Crema also listens to BetterDisplay in a mode the system cannot hold back while the app is inactive.
  • When Crema hands a key back to the system, its own bar stands down too — one keypress, one indicator, whichever order the two arrive in.
  • A failed mute read during volume-up no longer leaves the Mac muted while the bar keeps rising.
  • Clicks land exactly on the visible surface while it appears, matching hover — clicks in the dead margin no longer fall through — and a completed hover gesture no longer re-routes a later interaction in styles that commit immediately.
  • The menu stays its width when two or three indicators are suspended at once; the warning used to widen the whole menu.
  • A MacBook on its own can turn the player off. The Displays section, with its "Show the player here" switch, is now offered for every connected display, a lone built-in one included.
  • The Classic style now shows the track's total length; a disabled progress bar dims like the buttons beside it; a VoiceOver adjustment on a HUD bar no longer leaves it stuck; and a level the app itself asked for is never taken as a reading.

Improved

  • VoiceOver. Play/Pause is announced by name, the HUD's icon no longer repeats the slider's label, and each display's row in Settings is one group carrying the display's name.
  • Escape leaves the welcome tour and the Accessibility onboarding. The tour's Indicators step offers the grant right beside the switch, and no longer teaches a shortcut that only works with the menu open.
  • The Now Playing tab says when nothing is reporting.
  • Brazilian Portuguese revised to the system's own register throughout — sentence case in menus and buttons, and one name per concept in both languages: the surface is the player. The three style names — Notch, Card, Classic — are product names and stay in English.

Requirements: macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later · Apple Silicon or Intel.

Crema isn't notarized by Apple, so a fresh install needs one extra step on first launch — see First launch in the README. Updating from 1.2.0 or later happens on its own.
