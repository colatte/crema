Get to know Crema.

Now Playing

Music, at a glance.

When a song starts or changes, Crema shows it by the notch for a moment, then tucks it away. Click the notch and the whole player opens: the artwork, where you are in the song, and the controls you reach for.

Everything in reach

Play, pause, skip, and drag to any point in the song, without leaving what you're doing.

Shows up, then steps aside

A new track appears beside the notch for a moment. Prefer quiet? Keep the player hidden until you click.

Your browser, if you like

Music playing in Safari, Chrome and other browsers can show too. It's off until you turn it on.

Styles

Your notch. Your way.

Choose a style for every display at once, or give one display its own. On a Mac without a notch, Notch shows as Card.

Blends into the notch

Black on black, flush with the bezel, as if the hardware had always done it.

Indicators

Turn it up. Quietly.

Turn on one switch and Crema replaces the system's volume, brightness and keyboard backlight indicators with its own, in the style you picked. Quit Crema and the system's come back.

Where the sound goes

The volume indicator shows whether you're on AirPods, headphones or an AirPlay speaker.

Brightness, where you look

A brightness key acts on the display under your pointer, so you never dim the wrong screen.

Put it anywhere

On Card, the indicator can sit in one of eight places, as a thin line or filling the whole card. At the bottom, it stays clear of the Dock.

Off until you want it

Crema only takes over the indicators once you turn the switch on, and hands a key back to macOS whenever it can't act on it.

Works with BetterDisplay

With BetterDisplay 4.2.1 or newer, Crema shows the brightness indicator for an external display too.

Setup

Ready in a minute.

The first time you open Crema, a short tour shows it working on your own screen, asks for the one permission it uses, and lets you pick a style. Every step can be skipped.

Privacy

Nothing to sign up for.

No account, no analytics. Crema only notices keys like volume and brightness, never what you type.

Questions? Answers.

Is Crema free?

Yes. Crema is free and open source under the GPL-3.0, and its code is on GitHub.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac with macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon or Intel.

Does my Mac need a notch?

No. On a display without a notch, Notch shows as Card, a panel just below the menu bar. Classic works everywhere.

Why does it ask for Accessibility?

So it can notice the volume and brightness keys, show its own brightness indicator and, if you choose, replace the system's. It only notices those keys, never what you type. Without it, Crema still shows what's playing and its volume indicator.

What about external displays?

Each display can have its own style, and the player shows on the displays you switch on. For an external display's brightness, Crema works with BetterDisplay 4.2.1 or newer.

Why does macOS warn me when I first open it?

Crema isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS warns about an unidentified developer. Open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway, once. If there's no button, run this in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Crema.app
How do I install and update it?

Download the disk image, drag Crema into Applications and open it. It lives in the menu bar, not the Dock, and checks for updates itself.
